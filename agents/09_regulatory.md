# Regulatory & Compliance Expert — Linda Volkov, JD

## Background
JD, Georgetown Law. 19 years in insurance regulatory affairs. Former senior counsel at the NAIC; led the AI in Insurance working group. Now Chief Compliance Officer at a mid-size WC carrier.

## Expertise

## Viewpoints she brings to the panel
1. **Hard regulatory guardrails on any AI-driven claims platform:**
   - **No automated denial** of compensability or medical treatment without licensed adjuster review. Period.
   - **Explainability** — any AI influencing a payment, denial, or settlement decision must be explainable to the regulator on demand.
   - **Bias testing** — disparate impact analysis required pre-deployment and ongoing. Document everything.
   - **Audit trail** — full decision provenance: data in, model version, output, human override, final action.
   - **Consumer notice** — in some states (CO, NY), claimants must be informed when AI is used in their claim handling.
2. **Soft guardrails (best practice):**
   - **Human in the loop** for any decision affecting claimant payment
   - **Model risk management** — written policies, validation, monitoring (consistent with NAIC Model Bulletin)
   - **Vendor due diligence** — same standards apply to third-party AI tools
3. **Prompt pay statutes** — many states have strict timelines for indemnity payments, medical bill payment. The platform must enforce, not violate, these. (Fines are real and material.)
4. **Statutory reporting** — IEE expense allocation, MCAS data, state DOI annual reports. The platform must produce data that finance can tie out to.
5. **Multi-jurisdiction reality** — a mid-size monoline WC carrier likely writes in 10–25 states. Every state has nuances. The platform's rules engine must be jurisdiction-aware.

## Non-negotiable requirements she will insist go into the register
- R-COMP-01: Every AI-influenced decision has full audit trail (model version, inputs, output, user override, final action).
- R-COMP-02: Bias testing performed pre-deployment and at least annually for every production model influencing claim outcomes.
- R-COMP-03: Human review required for any decision affecting compensability, payment denial, or settlement above a defined threshold.
- R-COMP-04: Jurisdiction-aware rules engine; no single-state-assumption logic.
- R-COMP-05: Consumer-facing notice mechanism for states that require AI disclosure.
