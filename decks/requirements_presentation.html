The question on the table

Where is the $15–25M of run-rate savings hiding in our LAE?

01 / DIAGNOSIS

LAE is the largest controllable expense bucket

For monoline WC carriers in our band, LAE runs 11–14% of premium. Top-quartile carriers run 8–9%. That gap is the prize.

02 / OPPORTUNITY

The gap is recoverable — over time, with discipline

A well-executed three-year program reliably captures 2.0–2.5 points. On a $500M book, that's $10–12.5M annually, run-rate.

Why now

Three forces compress the window for action

FORCE 1

Industry margin pressure

WC pricing has softened; loss-cost pressure from rising medical inflation is real. Expense discipline is the only fully controllable margin lever.

FORCE 2

Regulatory clock on AI

The NAIC AI Model Bulletin is being adopted state by state. Carriers that build AI governance into the platform from day one avoid expensive retrofits.

FORCE 3

Talent constraint

Experienced WC adjusters are retiring faster than they are being trained. Automation is not optional; it is the staffing strategy.

Capability tiers

Tier 1 · 12-month payback levers

T1.1

Intelligent FNOL intake with severity scoring

Every FNOL gets a severity score within 24 hours, driving routing to the right desk before time is wasted on the wrong one.

T1.2

Automated medical-only claim triage

Target 30–40% of medical-only at near-touchless. The adjuster reviews and affirms in seconds, not hours. Conservative routing — never auto-denial.

T1.3

Document AI for medical records and FNOL artifacts

Extract structured fields once. Stop re-keying medical records, wage statements, and provider info across three systems.

T1.4

Vendor performance scorecard + rate-card enforcement

One canonical vendor master with rate cards. Invoices validated automatically. Quality and cost visible across all spend categories.

Capability tiers

Tier 2 · 18–24 month optimization layer

T2.1

Litigation propensity model + early settlement workflow

Predict the attorney-represented files within 30 days. Engage settlement strategy before legal posture hardens.

T2.2

Return-to-work orchestration

Coordinate the medical, employer, and adjuster work that turns lost-time into modified duty — the single biggest indemnity and LAE lever.

T2.3

Real-time medical bill review with AI anomaly detection

Catch upcoding, unbundling, and out-of-network charges as bills arrive — not in a quarterly leakage report.

T2.4

Dynamic caseload balancing

Rebalance work across adjusters based on complexity-weighted load, not raw open-count.

Capability tiers

Tier 3 · 24–36 month closed-loop optimization

T3.1

Provider outcome scoring → network steering

Score providers on duration, cost, and RTW outcomes. Steer claimants toward the high performers within network rules.

T3.2

Vendor billing fraud detection

Anomaly detection on DME, transportation, translation, surveillance invoices — the categories where leakage hides.

T3.3

Adjuster knowledge assistant (GenAI)

"What's been tried on this file?" answered in seconds. Built carefully — not autonomous decisions.

CLOSED LOOP  Outcomes from Tiers 1 and 2 train the models that power Tier 3. The platform compounds.

Compliance & guardrails

Non-negotiables — engineered in, not bolted on

  • Full audit trail for every AI-influenced decision (model version, inputs, outputs, human overrides, final action).
  • Bias testing pre-deployment and at-least-annual for every production model influencing claim outcomes.
  • Human review required for any compensability decision, payment denial, or settlement above defined threshold.
  • Jurisdiction-aware rules engine — no single-state-assumption logic.
  • Consumer AI disclosure where required (CO, NY today — more coming).
  • Prompt-pay statutes encoded and monitored in the rules engine. The platform cannot delay payments to chase efficiency.
RULE  No auto-denial. The platform recommends; a licensed adjuster decides. Statutory and reputational risk demand this.

What could derail this

Risks & mitigations

R1  Adjuster resistance to "the algorithm"

Mitigation: adjusters co-design the workflow; AI is positioned as decision support, never as a performance surveillance tool; compensation re-aligned around outcomes.

R2  Operating-model change underinvested

Mitigation: change-management headcount funded inside the program from day one — not as an afterthought. Carriers without it capture ~40% of benefit.

R3  Regulatory exposure from AI use

Mitigation: NAIC AI Model Bulletin governance built in from day one; bias testing, explainability, human-in-loop are NFRs, not afterthoughts.

R4  Integration complexity with claims core

Mitigation: event-driven integration patterns; we consume and emit, we do not transact through the core. Idempotent handlers throughout.

R5  Vendor lock-in on AI tooling

Mitigation: build the orchestration and decision layer; buy commodity AI components (document AI, OCR, bill review engines).

R6  Over-aggressive STP routing

Mitigation: conservative touchless ceiling (~30–40% of med-only); adjuster affirmation step retained for all routed claims.
