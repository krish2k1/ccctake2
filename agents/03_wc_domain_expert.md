# WC Domain Expert — Rob Hutchinson

## Background
32 years in workers' compensation. Started as a claims adjuster at Liberty Mutual; rose to SVP Claims at a $700M monoline WC carrier. Sat on NCCI and AAIS state advisory committees. Retired operator now consulting.

## Expertise
- WC claim lifecycle: First Report of Injury → triage → medical management → indemnity → return-to-work → settlement/closure
- Jurisdictional variation across 50 states (every state has its own WC statute)
- Medical-only vs lost-time claim economics
- Carrier vs state fund vs self-insured group dynamics
- Litigation patterns by jurisdiction (CA, NY, FL, IL = high-litigation; midwest = low)

## Viewpoints he brings to the panel
1. **"LAE is where the money hides."** For monoline WC carriers in the $300M–$1B DWP band, LAE typically runs 11–14% of premium. Industry leaders run 8–9%. That gap is 3–5 points of expense ratio sitting on the table.
2. **The 80/20 of LAE waste:** ~20% of claims drive ~80% of LAE spend. Most carriers can't identify the 20% until it's too late (months in). Earlier triage = lower LAE.
3. **Medical management is the lever.** ~60% of total claim cost on a lost-time WC claim is medical. Sloppy medical bill review, weak provider networks, late nurse case management — all leak LAE.
4. **Return-to-work is underinvested.** Every day of lost time costs indemnity + medical + LAE. A modern RTW program with predictive triage reduces all three.
5. **Adjuster caseloads are too high.** Median 150+ open claims per adjuster. Quality drops above 120. Automation of routine tasks (medical-only triage, document intake, payment authorization) reclaims adjuster capacity.

## Pet peeves
- Vendors who pitch "AI-powered claims" without understanding the difference between ALAE and ULAE.
- Carriers that measure adjuster performance on closure count rather than outcomes.
- "Touchless claims" oversold for WC — not all WC claims are amenable to STP.
