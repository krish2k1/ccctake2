The hidden failure inside today's claim handling

The same back-strain claim is handled three different ways across three of our desks.

DESK A

Nurse on day 7

Adjuster A's habit. Closes in 92 days. Outcome good. Cost: $11.4K.

DESK B

Nurse on day 60

Adjuster B's habit. Closes in 184 days. Worse outcome. Cost: $24.7K.

DESK C

No nurse

Adjuster C never refers. Treatment drifts. Attorney enters. Cost: $48.2K + ALAE.

There is no decision-support and no learning loop. Every adjuster picks services from memory and vendors from habit — across 36 service types and a sprawling vendor list that no one has ever scored.

Each service in the catalog is typed by how it gets decided. Compensability denial and surveillance never auto-route. Interpretation and MSA fire on hard rules. Nurse referral fires on a learned probability.

Side two — the vendor pool

8 eligibility gates. 6 scoring factors. 3 governance rules.

8 GATES · binary

Eligibility

  • State licensure current
  • E&O insurance at carrier limits
  • HIPAA BAA executed
  • Compliance attestations on file
  • No active regulatory action
  • SOC 2 Type II or equivalent
  • Machine-readable rate card filed
  • Capacity disclosed weekly
6 FACTORS · scored

Performance

  • Outcome 40% — propensity-adjusted
  • Cost 25% — savings-to-fee or cost-per-file
  • Speed 15% — SLA hit rate, cycle time
  • Specialty fit 10% — per-claim
  • Adjuster sat 5% — survey-based
  • Compliance 5% — DOI, EDI, audit
3 RULES · governance

Guardrails

  • Anti-concentration — max 50% to any one vendor
  • Exploration quota — 5% to ranks 4–5
  • Sample-size floor — 30 closures before published rank

Scoring corrects for selection bias. Top vendors get easier claims. Without correction, "good vendor" really means "lucky assignment." Propensity weighting controls for severity, litigation propensity, body part, jurisdiction, and venue.

THE RULES OF THE ROAD

Three rules earned from prior rollouts

  • Don't take the decision. Improve the decision. The adjuster picks.
  • Show your work. Every factor visible. Every comparable shown. Every override pattern surfaced.
  • Frictionless override. Two clicks max. Reason coded. Reason trended. Not punished.

First 12 months: individual adjuster override rate is not a performance metric. Adoption beats accuracy in year one.

Compliance is engineered in, not bolted on

Seven guardrails on the record

G1 · COMPENSABILITY

No automated denial

The layer recommends services. It never denies compensability or treatment. That stays with a licensed adjuster.

G2 · SURVEILLANCE

Human approval before sub-rosa

Surveillance recommendations require a named human approval. Privacy and state-law risk are real.

G3 · AUDIT

Every recommendation persisted

Snapshot, model version, weights, scores, adjuster action, outcome. Forever. Regulator-ready.

G4 · BIAS

Output-side bias testing

Disparate-impact analysis on vendor steering by claimant demographic. Pre-deployment and annually.

G5 · JURISDICTION

State-aware end-to-end

Vendor eligibility, scoring weights, consumer-notice copy — all configured per jurisdiction.

G6 · CONSUMER NOTICE

Honored where required

Colorado and New York currently require AI-use disclosure in claim handling. The platform supports it; we extend as states adopt.

Backed by the NAIC AI Model Bulletin's seven principles — fairness, accountability, governance, risk management, third-party oversight, transparency, data quality.

How we know it's working

Five numbers we publish quarterly

M1 · FINANCE

Counterfactual savings

Propensity-matched estimator on the 5% holdout cohort. The defensible number for finance and the board.

M2 · ADOPTION

Acceptance rate

% of recommendations accepted unchanged. Target ≥70% by end of Phase 2.

M3 · TIMING

Time-to-service

Median days from trigger to vendor engagement, by service. Late-NCM bucket measured directly.

M4 · OUTCOMES

Outcome lift per service

RTW days, indemnity+ALAE per closed claim, claimant satisfaction — vs. pre-rollout cohort.

M5 · COMPLIANCE

DOI complaint ratio

Leading indicator that we're not optimizing cost at the expense of fair handling. Watched monthly.

SUCCESS = ALL 5

Not just M1

A program that saves money but fails on M5 (complaints) or M2 (adoption) is not a success. The five must move together.

Build sequence — inside the Session-I phasing

PHASE 2 · Months 9–24

  • Propensity-corrected outcome scoring
  • Timing model on time-sensitive services
  • Anti-concentration + exploration quotas active
  • Override-pattern learning loop running monthly
  • Vendor API published; degraded path SLA enforced
  • Bias-testing pipeline on recommendation outputs
  • Quarterly counterfactual attribution to finance
  • Catalog expanded to long-tail services

Phase 3 (24–36) ties this layer to provider scoring and the closed-loop optimization defined in Session I — vendor outcomes inform provider scoring inform vendor steering. Compounding flywheel.
