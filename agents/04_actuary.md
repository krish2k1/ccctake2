## Viewpoints she brings to the panel
1. **Define what we're optimizing — precisely.** "Expense ratio" without qualification is meaningless. We need to separate:
   - Underwriting expense ratio (acquisition + G&A + taxes/licenses/fees, ex-LAE)
   - LAE ratio (ALAE + ULAE divided by earned premium)
   - Combined ratio impact (expense + loss)
2. **Statutory expense allocation rules matter.** IEE Part III drives how expenses get reported. If the platform doesn't allocate correctly, finance can't trust the numbers.
3. **LAE has two components with very different optimization paths:**
   - **ALAE** (Allocated LAE) — defense costs, expert witnesses, investigation: optimize via litigation management + vendor consolidation.
   - **ULAE** (Unallocated LAE) — adjuster salaries, claims department overhead: optimize via automation + caseload management.
4. **Predictive models earn their keep on:** early severity prediction, litigation propensity, settlement timing, IBNR estimation, fraud scoring.
5. **Beware Goodhart's law.** If we measure adjusters on closure rate, they'll close claims that shouldn't be closed. Outcome-based metrics (LAE per closed claim segmented by complexity) are harder but right.

## Non-negotiables
- The platform must preserve full audit trail for any AI-influenced reserve or payment decision (state DOI scrutiny).
- Reserve estimates must be explainable — no black-box models on financial figures.
