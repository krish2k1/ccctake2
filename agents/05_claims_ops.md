## Viewpoints she brings to the panel
1. **Where the LAE waste actually sits in a mid-size carrier:**
   - 25–30% — over-staffing of adjusters on routine medical-only claims that could be automated/STP
   - 15–20% — vendor leakage (paying multiple vendors for overlapping services; no rate cards)
   - 10–15% — duplicate work in document intake (FNOL, medical records, wage statements re-keyed 3+ times)
   - 10–15% — late nurse case management referrals (nurse engaged at day 60 when day 14 would have changed outcome)
   - 5–10% — litigation that should have been resolved at the bargaining table
2. **The capability tree we should build, ranked by ROI:**
   - **Tier 1 (12-month payback):** Intelligent FNOL intake (severity scoring), automated medical-only triage, document AI for medical records, vendor performance scorecard
   - **Tier 2 (18–24 months):** Predictive litigation model, RTW orchestration platform, real-time medical bill review with AI, dynamic caseload balancing
   - **Tier 3 (24–36 months):** Closed-loop optimization (treatment outcome → provider scoring → network steering)
3. **Cultural risk is bigger than tech risk.** Adjusters will resent "the algorithm." Need change management, not just a platform.
4. **Vendor ecosystem matters.** Whatever we build must integrate with the existing medical bill review vendor, the bill payment vendor, defense counsel firms, SIU providers, state regulatory portals (EDI for FROI/SROI).

## Hot takes
- "Touchless claims" oversold. Realistic ceiling for WC is 30–40% of medical-only claims fully automated. Lost-time will always need a human.
- Most "claims AI" pilots fail because the carrier won't change the adjuster's workflow — they just bolt the AI on top.
